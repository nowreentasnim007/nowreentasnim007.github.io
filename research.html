Research agenda

Three connected questions.

How can risk information become timely finance, usable warnings and defensible investment decisions?

Anticipatory financing and risk-informed finance

How should pre-arranged finance reflect livelihood losses, vulnerability and need before a riverine flood becomes a humanitarian crisis?

Methods and evidenceHousehold surveys, observational analysis, qualitative synthesis and contextual flood-model outputs; applied finance-design experience with climate-vulnerable enterprises.

Inclusive multi-hazard early warning

Who can receive, understand and act on a warning, and which institutional changes make early-warning systems usable for persons with disabilities and other excluded groups?

Methods and evidenceField observation, case stories, interviews, focus groups, disability-sensitive documentation, policy analysis and operational guidance.

Spatial climate-risk analysis for resilient infrastructure

How can spatial risk and economic appraisal distinguish highly exposed assets from adaptation investments that merit closer engineering and local verification?

Methods and evidenceArcGIS, QGIS, hazard–exposure–vulnerability mapping, infrastructure-systems modelling, Python at a basic level and adaptation-benefit assessment.

Doctoral research interests

Research interests centre on how interacting hazards affect infrastructure networks, ecosystems and livelihoods, and how interdependencies transmit direct damage into cascading disruption and indirect loss. Spatial and systems modelling of baseline networks, integrated with field evidence and timely risk data, offers a basis for examining these dynamics and identifying priorities for intervention. The intended focus is risk prioritisation, inclusive anticipatory financing and adaptive investment that protect livelihood assets, sustain essential services and strengthen disaster resilience across hazards and settings.
